# Nutrition planning v1

`nutrition-1.0` is a deterministic planning assistant, not a registered dietitian or a clinically validated nutrition prescription. `nutritionDay` in `public/engine.js` consumes the displayed cycling prescription and ready strength sessions. It does not infer medical status from Strava, copy ride records into nutrition storage, or send data to an AI service. Personal answers are entered through the local intake, not embedded in source code.

## Workflow and provenance

Choose Nutritionist, review the intake, then select a day. The menu has five meal events; meals 2–4 are portable by default. Weights refer to the food's stated edible condition. Ride fuel is additional to those events and counted exactly once in daily carbohydrate and energy. Breakfast is before morning riding; the editable default gap is 90 minutes, not an observed departure time. Dinner follows evening lifting on strength days. Actual product labels and cooking yields take priority over generic quantities.

The currently displayed accepted plan, or otherwise the current proposal, supplies duration and watt steps. Training source and proposal status are visible. Changes recalculate menus; no nutritional adherence or workout completion is inferred. Confirmed setup can supply proposed strength sessions until a strength week is accepted. Unknown training is not a rest day. Missing watts produce no cycling/day energy estimate; food portions are then illustrative and visibly flagged. Synthetic training remains labelled synthetic.

Intake is optional profile data saved in browser localStorage, included only in deliberate local exports, and removed by existing local-data deletion. It is not encrypted; use a private device. Derived menus remain in memory. Existing integration credentials stay server-side and are unaffected. The separate unresolved Strava API analytics/coaching restrictions documented in README still apply.

## Evidence and product decisions

- Carbohydrate follows broad workload-dependent ranges: 3–5, 5–7, or 6–10 g/kg/day. The duration bands and next-long-ride adjustment are explicit prototype choices. The [USADA nutrition guide](https://www.usada.org/durl/nutrition-guide-pdf/) and [joint sports-nutrition position](https://drugfreesport.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Position-stand-on-Nutrition-Athletic-Performance-ACSM-2016-1.pdf) support periodising intake, not these exact menus or an optimal individual dose.
- Protein reference is 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day; the portion solver initially aims at 1.8. Ordinary foods can exceed the reference as total portions rise. This is a guide, not a diagnosed need or ceiling. Portions, 25 g starch increments, food selection and iteration caps are product decisions.
- During rides: easy sessions of at most 60 minutes default to no mandatory carbohydrate. Longer easy, quality and long sessions start at 45, 60 and 75 g/hour respectively, always capped by supplied tolerance. Unknown tolerance starts at 30. Above 60, use a tolerated multiple-transportable carbohydrate mix. Published higher intakes do not establish individual tolerance. Fuel sources must be ovo-lacto compatible and their label totals must include both drinks and food.
- Fluid starts at 400–800 mL/hour as a flexible planning range, [consistent with public sports nutrition guidance](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/sporting-performance-and-food). Thirst, environment, sweat and tolerance govern adjustment; it is not a drinking quota. No sweat-sodium loss is invented. Sodium-containing drinks can be considered for long/hot work; there is no automatic salt-pill dose.
- Consented resting-energy estimation uses the [Ten Haaf equation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4183531/) only within its studied adult age cohort, 18–35 years: `11.936 × kg + 587.728 × height_metres − 8.129 × age + 191.027 × male_coefficient + 29.279`. The user can avoid this equation or supply an assessed non-training maintenance estimate. [Athlete equation comparisons](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10687135/) show substantial individual uncertainty. No lean-mass or energy-availability calculation is invented.
- The resting estimate times 1.4 is a provisional non-training allowance, not a measured activity factor. Prescribed work is `sum(watts × seconds)/1000` kJ; gross cycling energy assumes 24% efficiency and divides by `4.184 × 0.24`. Subtract the baseline allowance during cycling to reduce double counting. [Cycling efficiency research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35253274/) indicates variation; this parameter is not an individual measurement. Gym energy adds a rough net allowance of `2.5 × kg × hours`; lifting density is unknown. Food and ride-fuel energy are intake, never added again as expenditure.
- Gradual loss considers only a 150 kcal model adjustment on confirmed non-training days, excluding the day before a long ride. No automated adjustment applies to hard/long/double days or an expired goal. This small adjustment and the >0.25 kg/week review trigger are conservative product rules, not validated universal thresholds. The goal date is a review target; no deadline guarantee, calorie ceiling, dehydration strategy, or aggressive catch-up deficit is generated. Review actual hunger, recovery, performance and weight trends with a sports dietitian before relying on the estimate.

## Vegetarian coverage and supplements

Eggs, dairy, legumes, calcium-set tofu, grains, vegetables, fruit, walnuts and ground flax provide a varied starting pattern. [Health Canada's vegetarian guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/explore/dietary-guidelines/applying/considerations-vegetarian-diets.html) informs the review prompts. Generic calcium, iron, B12 and vitamin D estimates are displayed; iodine, zinc, omega-3 conversion, bioavailability and the entire dietary pattern are not assessed. A food total does not diagnose adequate status or deficiency.

The [AIS supplement framework](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements) and [IOC supplement consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29589768/) support decisions based on need, context and risk. No supplement is automatically dosed or included in totals. Sports foods can supply planned ride fuel; protein powder is a convenience. B12 needs reliable intake; vitamin D needs individual assessment. Iron is not added without appropriate assessment and, when indicated, blood tests. Creatine is an optional strength/repeated-sprint discussion with a water-weight caveat; caffeine requires sleep, tolerance, medications and existing intake review. No current supplement list is assumed empty. Independent batch testing reduces but cannot eliminate contamination risk.

## Food dataset

Twenty generic foods were extracted from the official [USDA SR Legacy CSV dataset, April 2018](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/) on November 8, 2026. `docs/NUTRITION-FOODS.json` contains the selected FDC IDs, original descriptions and per-100 g nutrient values; the runtime catalog is embedded in `engine.js`. No API key is used. Original USDA energy values are retained rather than recalculated with 4/4/9. Missing nutrient fields remain unknown. Fortified milk, calcium-set tofu and recipes vary substantially by brand. Cooked rice/pasta/legumes shopping weights need conversion using the rider's cooking yield.

Chill and transport portable perishable meals safely; see [Health Canada's packed-meal advice](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/seasonal-food-safety/safe-school-lunches.html). Allergies, intolerances, clinical restrictions, low weight inputs, exclusions and weight-gain prescriptions pause automatic menus pending review. This prototype has not received qualified clinical or sports-dietitian review and is not ready for unrestricted production nutrition advice.

## Verification

`npm test` covers nutrient arithmetic, one-time fuel counting, tolerance caps, watt-step energy snapshots, baseline double-count prevention, lifting, protected training-day fueling, missing watts/schedules, equation consent/age, restriction gates and intake persistence without derived-menu storage. `npm run check` checks JavaScript syntax. These verify software behavior, not physiological effectiveness. Manual UI review covers intake saving, restored training settings, daily navigation and long-ride portions.
